Recreate your treadmill session as segments — or skip this tab and record it live instead. Each segment becomes a lap in the FIT file.
by picks which two you type: time + speed, dist + speed, or dist + time — where the speed is worked out for you. hill picks whether you type the grade in % or the climb in m. Greyed italic values are the ones TreadLab calculates; change a dropdown to swap which is which. Negative grades are allowed for decline. power and cad are optional — fill them in and the figure is written to every second of that segment, which is how you log a ride you only remember the averages for (e.g. 11.3 km, 75 W, 80 rpm). Leave them blank and they're omitted entirely. Set the activity to Virtual ride on the export tab.

Then upload at strava.com/upload. Tip: make your first upload private and check the elevation shows up the way you want — “Virtual run” is what Zwift uses and reliably keeps file elevation; some platforms zero out elevation on activities flagged “treadmill”.

Map loop draws your run as a closed loop in verified-empty ocean, sized so the loop equals your distance — purely so Strava shows a map. Near Watopia (12°S 166°E) parks it in the same South Pacific neighbourhood where Zwift georeferences Watopia, 80+ km clear of any land, road or Zwift island. Point Nemo (49°S 123°W) is the oceanic pole of inaccessibility — 2,700 km from the nearest land, where retired spacecraft are deorbited. Pick no map for a plain activity. Either way the track can’t touch real-world segments or leaderboards.
